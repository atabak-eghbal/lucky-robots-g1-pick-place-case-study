The Starting Line

The project began with a manual keyboard demo for the Unitree G1. While it demonstrated that the underlying ONNX policies for walking and reaching were capable, it lacked the sequencing and decision-making required for autonomy.

Our challenge was to bridge the gap between "capable policies" and a "robust task machine"—while ensuring the system remains debuggable and stable.

The Goal: Robust Autonomy

We delivered a 12-state Finite-State Machine (FSM) that coordinates locomotion and manipulation concurrently. The system handles:

  • Dynamic Approach: Walking to the cylinder with staircase speed scaling.
  • Precision Reaching: Managing a 12cm reacher accuracy floor via empirical thresholds.
  • Safe Transport: Maintaining carry poses during 180-degree navigation turns.
  • Reliable Release: Preventing simulation crashes through delayed physics restoration.

Why This Problem is Hard

  • Policy Distribution Shift: If the arm isn't in its expected carry pose, the walker's observations drift, leading to unstable gaits.
  • Coordinate Invariants: Reaching from a walking base requires transforming world-frame goals into pelvis-frame targets at every tick.
  • The Kinematic-to-Physical Gap: Releasing an object in simulation is a discrete event that can trigger explosive contact forces if not handled with temporal delays.
