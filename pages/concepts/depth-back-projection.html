Depth Back-Projection

Depth back-projection converts a pixel and depth value into a 3D point.

What it means

A camera image gives a pixel location, and a depth map tells how far that pixel is from the camera. Camera intrinsics map the pixel into a 3D ray, and back-projection estimates the object position in camera space.

Why it matters

Back-projection is the bridge from perception to control. It turns image-space detections into 3D coordinates that the reacher can use.

How it appears in this repo

The Visual Oracle uses segmentation to find a pixel on the cylinder and back-projects it using calibrated intrinsics to estimate the source pose. 

x = (u - cx) * z / fx
y = (v - cy) * z / fy
z = depth

Design implications

  • Camera calibration and intrinsics must stay in sync with the simulator.
  • Frame transforms are required to move from camera to world/pelvis space.
  • Depth sampling must be robust to partial occlusion.

Common failure modes or misunderstandings

  • Wrong camera intrinsics.
  • Wrong frame convention.
  • Sampling depth from the wrong pixel.
  • Object partially outside the frame.
  • Depth discontinuities at object edges.
