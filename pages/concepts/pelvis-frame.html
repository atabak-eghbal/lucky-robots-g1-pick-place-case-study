Pelvis-Frame Target

A pelvis-frame target describes where the robot hand should move relative to the robot pelvis, not the world.

What it means

The reacher expects targets in the robot’s body/pelvis frame. A world-space point on the table must be transformed into pelvis coordinates before the policy can use it.

Why it matters

The robot moves while reaching. Pelvis-frame targets keep the policy stable by describing the goal relative to the moving body instead of a fixed world coordinate.

How it appears in this repo

  • Source and target world points are converted into pelvis coordinates.
  • Bias and clipping keep targets inside the right-arm workspace.
  • Targets are sent to the right reacher every tick.

Design implications

  • Every world-space goal needs a reliable frame transform.
  • Axis conventions must be documented and tested.
  • Workspace checks prevent unreachable targets.

Common failure modes or misunderstandings

  • Sending world coordinates directly to the reacher.
  • Wrong quaternion/rotation convention.
  • Target outside arm workspace.
  • Misunderstanding x/y/z axes.
