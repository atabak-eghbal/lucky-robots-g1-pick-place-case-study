Walker Policy

The walker policy is the learned controller that produces body/joint commands for locomotion.

What it means

walker.onnx maps robot state and velocity commands to joint actions. It expects a specific observation layout and produces the base gait and turning behavior.

Why it matters

Locomotion stability determines whether the arm can reach and grasp at all. The walker policy is the foundation of the entire pick-and-place sequence.

How it appears in this repo

  • The controller builds a 99D observation each tick.
  • Arm joint positions are part of the observation.
  • The model already includes internal normalization.

Design implications

  • Observation order must match training.
  • Arm behavior feeds back into walking stability.
  • Action scaling must stay consistent with config.

Common failure modes or misunderstandings

  • Wrong observation order.
  • Double-normalizing input.
  • Wrong action scaling.
  • Arm joints outside expected distribution.
  • Aggressive velocity commands.
